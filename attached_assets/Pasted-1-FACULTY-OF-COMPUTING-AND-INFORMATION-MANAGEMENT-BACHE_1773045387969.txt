CHAPTER ONE: INTRODUCTION
1.1 Background Information
In recent years, the proliferation of wireless internet service providers (WISPs) has significantly 
increased demand for reliable and flexible WiFi services. Kingstone WiFi Solutions is one such provider 
offering Hotspot, PPPoE, and Static IP access to residential, commercial, and institutional customers. As 
subscriber bases grow and data usage increases, so does the complexity of managing billing, customer 
accounts, network access, and above all, security. Billing systems for ISPs, especially those handling 
sensitive usage and payment data, are critical components of the operational infrastructure; yet many of 
them remain vulnerable to security threats such as unauthorized access, billing manipulation, data 
breaches, and weak authentication (EaseBill Global, 2025; Why Use ISP Billing System…, 2025).
Hotspot, PPPoE (Point‐to‐Point Protocol over Ethernet), and Static IP authentication modes each 
introduce specific security challenges. For example, PPPoE, though widely used, has documented 
vulnerabilities—such as the potential to impersonate PPPoE authentication servers and recover 
passwords when using insecure authentication protocols like PAP or weak variants of CHAP (On the 
Security of PPPoE Network, 2020). This problem is exacerbated when billing systems and network 
infrastructure do not enforce encryption and proper access controls. With Hotspot access, there is also 
risk of data interception, cookie or credential theft, and man‐in‐the‐middle attacks if login portals, 
captive portals, or transmission channels are unsecured (Exploring the Security of Mobile Hotspots: 
Risks and Best Practices, n.d.).
Moreover, stakeholders in ISP billing systems report that lack of strong authentication, absent or weak 
audit trails, and unencrypted storage of customer and financial data are among the most frequent sources 
of security incidents (MobiLink WiFi Billing System, n.d.; Best ISP Billing Software in Kenya, 2025). 
These vulnerabilities not only expose the ISP to financial loss—through billing fraud or unauthorized 
resiliency modifications—but also to reputational damage and potential regulatory penalties.
Globally, data breaches have far‐reaching consequences. A study on the cost of data breaches found that 
malicious attacks which involved stolen or compromised credentials led to significantly higher financial 
losses than system glitches or human error, with companies paying on average millions of dollars in 
incident response, notification, and remediation (The Cost of Data Breaches, 2020). For an ISP, the 
6
damage to customer trust following a breach of billing information can lead to churn, legal liabilities, 
and considerable operational disruption.
Locally, in Kenya and similar regions, ISPs are increasingly integrating advanced billing systems that 
support Hotspot, PPPoE, and Static IP, along with features such as real‐time usage tracking, automated 
invoicing, and multiple payment gateways (Lipanet, 2025; Mobilink WiFi Billing System, n.d.; EaseBill 
Global, 2025). However, many do not yet fully integrate security features such as end‐to‐end encryption 
of data at rest and in transit, multi‐factor authentication, continuous monitoring of user behavior, and 
robust role‐based access control. These gaps remain a key concern for ISPs seeking to scale operations 
while safeguarding user data and revenue streams.
Given this environment, Kingstone WiFi Solutions must ensure that its billing system is not only 
functionally capable of handling multiple authentication modes (Hotspot, PPPoE, Static IP) but that it 
also embeds security at its core. This includes secure protocols for authentication, encrypted 
communication channels, proper key management, secure storage of sensitive data, detailed logging for 
audit, mechanisms for intrusion detection or alerting, and effective user and access management.
The academic and industry literature underscores that a secure billing platform is not a luxury but a 
necessity for modern ISPs (EaseBill Global, 2025; Best ISP Billing Software…, 2025). Without such 
measures, ISPs risk financial leakage, operational downtime, loss of customer trust, and even legal 
exposure due to non-compliance with data protection laws. Thus, the Kingstone WiFi Billing System 
project, focused on security for the billing system, aims to address these issues directly by designing and 
implementing a secure, integrated solution supporting Hotspot, PPPoE, and Static IP authentication, 
with strong safeguards across authentication, encryption, audit, and access control.
1.2. Problem Statement
In the current digital age, reliable and secure Wi-Fi services have become an essential part of daily 
operations for individuals and organizations alike. Internet Service Providers (ISPs) rely heavily on 
efficient billing systems to manage user subscriptions, monitor data usage, and ensure consistent 
revenue collection. However, as service demand and user data volumes increase, many small ISPs face 
growing challenges in maintaining both operational efficiency and data security within their billing 
systems.
7
Despite their importance, most existing billing systems used by local ISPs are either semi-automated or 
inadequately secured.The consequences of these vulnerabilities are significant. Unauthorized system 
access or manipulation can lead to inaccurate billing, loss of revenue, and potential data breaches, which 
ultimately undermine customer confidence and the company’s reputation. In a competitive ISP market, 
such weaknesses may also lead to regulatory non-compliance and reduced client retention.
While there are various commercial billing platforms available, most are either too costly or not tailored 
to the security needs of small and medium-sized ISPs operating in localized environments. This creates a 
technological gap where affordable, customizable, and secure billing systems are lacking.
Therefore, there is a need to design and implement a secure Wi-Fi billing system that integrates strong 
user authentication, data encryption, access control, and real-time monitoring features. Such a system 
will help ensure data integrity, protect sensitive information, and enhance both operational and financial 
security for the organization.
1.3. Proposed Solution
The proposed Kingstone WiFi Billing System (KWBS) will serve as a secure and automated platform 
designed to efficiently manage all customer billing operations while incorporating advanced 
cybersecurity mechanisms. The system will employ strong user authentication and role-based access 
control to ensure that only authorized individual such as administrators, billing officers, and customers
can access specific system functionalities. To safeguard sensitive information, all billing and payment 
data will be encrypted both during storage and transmission, effectively preventing unauthorized access 
or data breaches. Secure payment processing will be achieved through the integration of trusted payment 
gateways such as M-Pesa APIs, ensuring that financial transactions are protected from interception or 
fraud. Additionally, the system will include detailed audit logging to track every user action, thereby 
enhancing accountability and simplifying the detection of suspicious activities. Automated billing 
features will enable the system to generate monthly invoices based on actual usage, minimizing human 
error and administrative workload. A real-time dashboard will provide live updates on payment statuses, 
customer accounts, and overall system activity, supporting timely and informed decision-making. The 
entire solution will be developed using secure coding practices and hosted on a cloud platform fortified 
with multi-layered security protections to guarantee data integrity, confidentiality, and availability.

CHAPTER TWO: LITERATURE REVIEW
2.1 Security Vulnerabilities in Existing Billing Systems 
Research consistently shows that billing systems are vulnerable to numerous security threats, including 
weak authentication, insufficient encryption, poor access control, and absence of audit trails. Such 
vulnerabilities expose systems to data manipulation, unauthorized access, and potential privacy 
breaches.
Lu (2022), in Encryption Management of Accounting Data Based on DES Algorithm of Wireless Sensor 
Network, found that inadequate encryption and weak key management enable data exposure and 
manipulation in open networks. Although the study focused on accounting systems, the findings are 
directly applicable to billing data, where confidentiality and integrity are essential. Lu emphasized that 
symmetric encryption methods like DES are outdated unless enhanced through secure key distribution 
or chaotic key generation.
Similarly, Emersion (n.d.), in Security Features in Billing Automation Systems, reported that many 
billing systems lack core features such as robust role-based access control (RBAC), secure payment 
gateways, and end-to-end encryption. Even when present, these features are often inconsistently 
implemented.
Chen, Jan, and Chen (2009), in Fair and Secure Mobile Billing Systems, identified major weaknesses 
including the absence of non-repudiation, insecure billing transactions, and trust dependency on third￾party observers. They proposed the use of hash chain mechanisms and digital signatures to maintain 
fairness and data integrity, techniques that are equally relevant to ISP billing systems such as Hotspot 
and PPPoE models.
A more privacy-focused approach is presented in Im, Kwon, Jeon, and Lee (2019) in Privacy￾Preserving Electricity Billing System Using Functional Encryption. Their findings showed that 
encrypting consumption data not only prevents data leaks but also enables accurate billing without 
revealing sensitive usage information.
In summary, existing billing systems suffer from common vulnerabilities:
 Weak authentication and authorization
10
 Poor encryption and key management
 Lack of audit logs and data integrity verification
 Susceptibility to data manipulation
 Absence of non-repudiation mechanisms
For Kingstone WiFi Solutions, these weaknesses are amplified due to multiple connection modes 
(Hotspot, PPPoE, Static IP), each introducing distinct entry points for security threats in login portals, 
routers, and storage layers.
2.2 Modern Secure Billing Systems Used by ISPs and Their Security Features
Contemporary ISP billing systems demonstrate evolving trends in security, emphasizing encryption, 
authentication, and fraud prevention.
ISP Kenya (n.d.) provides an ISP Billing System supporting Hotspot, PPPoE, and Static IP services. It 
integrates features like authentication, network monitoring, and customer management. While the exact 
encryption and audit details are not disclosed, the inclusion of these functions signifies increased 
awareness of cybersecurity in Kenya’s ISP sector.
Orbitlink Solutions (n.d.) focuses on fraud prevention, encryption, and secure payment integration. Their 
system ensures that customer data is encrypted both in transit and at rest, incorporating fraud detection 
mechanisms that flag suspicious usage patterns — aligning with best practices found in academic 
literature.
In academia, Erdayandi, Cordeiro, and Mustafa (2023) proposed a Privacy-Preserving and Accountable 
Billing Protocol for Peer-to-Peer Energy Trading Markets that leverages homomorphic encryption and 
blockchain to ensure privacy, transparency, and non-repudiation. Although developed for the energy 
sector, these principles can enhance ISP billing systems by improving auditability and accountability.
Ibrahem et al. (2020) introduced a Privacy-Preserving Electricity Theft Detection with Dynamic Billing 
and Load Monitoring for AMI Networks framework that combines functional encryption with fraud 
detection algorithms. This design maintains data privacy while enabling real-time usage monitoring and 
anomaly detection — a valuable model for ISPs managing user data securely.
From these studies and industry systems, the most effective security features include:
11
 Multi-factor and role-based authentication
 End-to-end encryption (at rest and in transit)
 Comprehensive audit logging
 Real-time anomaly and fraud detection
 Secure payment gateways
 Blockchain-based transparency and non-repudiation
These practices establish a foundation for the next-generation billing system envisioned for Kingstone 
WiFi Solutions.
2.3 Designing a Secure Billing System for Kingstone WiFi Solutions
Drawing from both academic and industrial research, the proposed system aims to implement role-based 
authentication, encryption and secure communication, real-time monitoring, automated alerts, dashboard 
visualization, and compliance with cybersecurity standards.
2.3.1 Role-Based Authentication (RBAC)
RBAC ensures that users can access only the resources relevant to their roles, enforcing the principle of 
least privilege. Emersion (n.d.) highlights RBAC as a key feature for limiting unauthorized actions. 
Chen et al. (2009) further demonstrate that combining digital signatures and observer roles enforces 
accountability and non-repudiation within billing environments.
2.3.2 Encryption and Secure Communication
According to Investopedia (n.d.), secure systems should employ symmetric encryption (e.g., AES-256) 
for large data volumes and asymmetric encryption for secure key exchange and authentication. 
Transport Layer Security (TLS) and Secure Socket Layer (SSL) protocols protect data in transit.
The Revenue Cycle Management (RCM) literature in healthcare also supports end-to-end encryption as 
a standard for confidentiality and compliance (Ingeneis, n.d.). Further, functional encryption techniques 
(Im et al., 2019; Ibrahem et al., 2020) enable computation on encrypted billing data, enhancing both 
security and privacy.
12
2.3.3 Real-Time Monitoring and Automated Alerts
Real-time monitoring enhances both operational awareness and security responsiveness. Orbitlink 
Solutions (n.d.) emphasizes continuous usage monitoring and fraud detection to safeguard against 
anomalies. Similarly, energy billing systems studied by Ibrahem et al. (2020) integrate automated alerts 
that respond to suspicious or abnormal behavior patterns.
2.3.4 Dashboard and Secure Real-Time Access
Dashboards offer administrators and clients comprehensive visibility into billing activities. The Alternet 
ISP Management Platform (n.d.) provides tools for managing Hotspot and PPPoE sessions, monitoring 
usage, and generating reports. A secure dashboard for Kingstone WiFi should include role-based 
visibility, encryption-backed communication, and real-time analytics for transparency.
2.3.5 Usability, Efficiency, and Compliance
Security mechanisms must remain user-friendly to avoid poor user practices. Lu (2022) notes that 
overcomplex encryption or access systems often lead to usability challenges that compromise security. 
Therefore, Kingstone WiFi’s billing platform must balance security, performance, and ease of use, 
ensuring compliance with data protection and cybersecurity regulations.
2.4 Synthesis and Research Gaps
The reviewed literature and systems reveal substantial progress toward secure billing architectures. 
However, several gaps remain:
 Limited application of advanced cryptography (e.g., functional encryption, blockchain) in ISP 
billing
 Inconsistent implementation of audit logs and non-repudiation measures
 Insufficient integration of automated alerts and anomaly detection
For Kingstone WiFi Solutions, this represents an opportunity to develop a next-generation secure billing 
system that integrates:
 Comprehensive RBAC and multi-factor authentication
13
 End-to-end encryption and secure communication
 Real-time monitoring and fraud detection
 Secure, user-centric dashboards
 Compliance with cybersecurity standards
 Exploration of blockchain or functional encryption for accountability and privacy

This section describes the research methodology adopted to guide the design, development, and 
validation of the Kingstone WiFi Billing System (KWBS). The study employs an applied research

ensure that the system is grounded in real-world needs and validated through empirical data.
3.1.1 Research Design
The research adopts a descriptive and applied research design. The descriptive aspect focuses on 
understanding and documenting the weaknesses and challenges of the existing WiFi billing process at 
Kingstone WiFi Solutions. The applied aspect seeks to use these findings to design and develop a new 
system that addresses identified problems while incorporating security, usability, and performance 
improvements.
This dual approach enables the researcher to combine theoretical principles with practical 
implementation, ensuring that the final solution is both academically sound and operationally effective.

3.2. Development methodology
18
This project will employ the Secure Software Development Lifecycle (SSDLC) methodology, a 
structured approach that integrates security considerations throughout every stage of software 
development. Unlike traditional development models, which often treat security as an afterthought, the 
SSDLC embeds security requirements and practices from the planning phase through design, 
implementation, testing, and deployment. This approach ensures that vulnerabilities are identified and 
mitigated early, reducing the likelihood of cyber threats, unauthorized access, or data breaches in the 
final system. The methodology emphasizes confidentiality, integrity, and availability (CIA) — the three 
pillars of information security — and ensures compliance with global best practices such as the ISO/IEC 
27001 information security standard and the OWASP Top Ten security principles. The development of 
the Kingstone WiFi Billing System (KWBS) will follow seven key stages: planning and requirement 
gathering, system analysis, system design, development, testing, deployment, and documentation with 
training. Each of these stages is critical for ensuring that the system not only meets its functional 
requirements but is also secure, efficient, and user-friendly.
3.2.1. Planning and Requirement Gathering
The planning and requirement-gathering stage lays the foundation for the entire project. It involves 
identifying the key objectives, defining the system’s purpose, and understanding the specific security 
and functional requirements of the billing system. This phase begins with stakeholder engagement, 
including interviews and questionnaires directed at Kingstone WiFi Solutions’ IT staff, billing officers, 
network technicians, and customers. These interactions will help identify the weaknesses of the existing 
billing process, such as weak authentication, data exposure, lack of encryption, and absence of audit 
trails.
To ensure that the system meets both business and security needs, requirements will be categorized into 
functional, non-functional, and security-specific components. Functional requirements include features 
such as automated billing, payment integration, and real-time dashboards. Non-functional requirements 
focus on performance, usability, and scalability, while security requirements encompass user 
authentication, data encryption, and logging. Threat modeling techniques, such as STRIDE (Spoofing, 
Tampering, Repudiation, Information Disclosure, Denial of Service, and Elevation of Privilege), will be 
applied to anticipate potential threats and define mitigation strategies early in the project. The outcome 
of this phase will be a comprehensive System Requirement Specification (SRS) document that guides all 
subsequent development phases.
19
3.2.2. System Analysis
In the system analysis phase, the functional and security requirements identified in the planning phase 
are examined in greater depth to define the system’s scope and architecture. The goal is to transform the 
requirements into a structured framework that describes how the system should operate and how security 
controls will be integrated. This phase includes the development of data flow diagrams (DFDs), entity￾relationship diagrams (ERDs), and use-case diagrams to visually represent the flow of information, user 
interactions, and system processes.
Security analysis forms the core of this stage. It involves identifying critical assets such as user 
credentials, payment data, and billing records and mapping out potential attack surfaces. Techniques 
such as risk assessment and vulnerability analysis will be applied to evaluate potential risks in data 
transmission, authentication, and system configuration. The results will guide the design of access 
control mechanisms, encryption protocols, and system privileges.
Another critical activity in this phase is defining user roles and permissions. The system will incorporate 
Role-Based Access Control (RBAC), ensuring that users have access only to resources necessary for 
their role. For instance, administrators will have full access to system management tools, billing officers 
will handle transactions and reports, and customers will only view their account information and 
payment history. The analysis stage also includes defining data classification policies, ensuring that 
sensitive data such as passwords and financial details are stored and transmitted securely.
3.2.3. System Design
The system design phase translates analytical insights into a detailed architectural blueprint for 
development. This phase will focus on creating a secure, scalable, and modular architecture that 
integrates the required features and enforces the highest standards of cybersecurity. Unified Modeling 
Language (UML) diagrams, including class diagrams, sequence diagrams, and component diagrams, 
will be developed to illustrate system components, interactions, and dependencies. The design will 
adhere to the principles of secure architecture, emphasizing defense in depth, least privilege, and fail￾safe defaults.
A key aspect of this phase is designing a secure database schema using PostgreSQL. Sensitive data 
fields — including passwords, customer details, and billing information — will be encrypted using the 
Advanced Encryption Standard (AES-256) algorithm. Passwords will be hashed using algorithms such 
20
as bcrypt to prevent reverse-engineering attacks. The communication between the client and server will 
be secured using HTTPS with SSL/TLS certificates, ensuring encrypted transmission of data.
The system design will also specify the implementation of secure APIs for integration with third-party 
services such as M-Pesa and PayPal. These APIs will use token-based authentication (JWT) to prevent 
unauthorized access. Security mechanisms such as firewalls, input validation, and Cross-Site Request 
Forgery (CSRF) protection will be incorporated to prevent common attacks like SQL injection, cross￾site scripting (XSS), and session hijacking. In addition, logging and auditing mechanisms will be 
designed to track user activities for compliance and forensic analysis. Finally, the design phase will 
consider scalability and maintainability, ensuring that the system can accommodate future updates and a 
growing customer base.
3.2.4. Development
The development phase involves the actual construction of the system based on the approved design 
specifications. Development will follow secure coding practices to prevent the introduction of 
vulnerabilities. The backend will be developed using Python (Django framework), chosen for its robust 
security features, such as built-in protection against SQL injection, XSS, and CSRF attacks. Django also 
facilitates secure authentication systems and session management. The frontend will be developed using 
React.js, enabling a responsive and interactive user interface that integrates seamlessly with the backend 
through secure RESTful APIs.
The PostgreSQL database will serve as the primary data storage solution, offering reliability, scalability, 
and strong security features. All data transactions between the frontend, backend, and database will be 
encrypted using SSL/TLS protocols. Source code will be maintained in a GitHub repository for version 
control and collaborative development. Code reviews will be conducted regularly to ensure compliance 
with secure coding standards, and static code analysis tools (such as Bandit and SonarQube) will be used 
to detect potential security flaws before deployment.
Furthermore, the development process will follow an iterative approach, allowing for continuous 
feedback and improvement. Each iteration will produce a working module that can be tested and 
validated against both functional and security requirements. This agile-like approach will ensure that 
vulnerabilities are detected early and addressed promptly. Development will also include the creation of 
configuration scripts for deploying the system in a secure environment, ensuring that default passwords, 
open ports, and unnecessary services are eliminated before release.
21
3.2.5. Testing
Testing is one of the most critical phases in the SSDLC, as it validates that the system meets all 
functional and security requirements. This project will employ a multi-layered testing strategy, including 
functional testing, security testing, and user acceptance testing (UAT).
Functional testing will verify that all system modules — authentication, billing, reporting, payment 
processing, and dashboards — operate as intended. Unit and integration tests will be performed to 
ensure that components interact correctly.
Security testing will include vulnerability assessments and penetration testing. Tools such as OWASP 
ZAP and Burp Suite will be used to identify and exploit potential weaknesses, including SQL injection, 
XSS, insecure direct object references, and broken authentication. Static and dynamic analysis will 
detect security vulnerabilities in both code and runtime environments. Additionally, the system will be 
subjected to stress and load testing to assess its performance under high demand and detect any potential 
denial-of-service vulnerabilities.
User Acceptance Testing (UAT) will involve real users — administrators, billing officers, and 
customers — interacting with the system to ensure it meets their needs and usability expectations. 
During this phase, testers will validate the clarity of workflows, responsiveness of interfaces, and 
reliability of billing calculations. Any discovered defects or usability issues will be documented and 
addressed before deployment. Security retesting will follow every major change to ensure no new 
vulnerabilities are introduced.
3.2.6. Deployment
Deployment marks the transition of the system from the development environment to a live operational 
setting. The Kingstone WiFi Billing System will be hosted securely on a cloud platform such as Amazon 
Web Services (AWS) or Google Cloud Platform (GCP), both of which provide advanced security 
infrastructure and compliance with global security standards. The cloud environment will be configured 
with network segmentation, virtual private clouds (VPCs), and firewall rules to restrict unauthorized 
access. Secure Shell (SSH) keys will be used for administrative access instead of passwords, and all 
deployment processes will follow the principle of least privilege.
SSL certificates will be installed to secure communication between clients and servers, while automated 
backup and recovery mechanisms will be configured to ensure data availability in case of failure or 
attack. Continuous monitoring tools will be deployed to track system performance, detect anomalies, 
22
and generate real-time alerts. Logs from the system, database, and network layers will be centralized for 
analysis and stored securely. Regular updates and patch management will be performed to protect 
against newly discovered vulnerabilities.
To prevent insider threats, multi-factor authentication (MFA) will be enforced for administrative 
accounts, and security group policies will be implemented to manage permissions and access levels. A 
disaster recovery plan will be established, detailing procedures for restoring system functionality in the 
event of a cyberattack or hardware failure. Before going live, a final security audit will be conducted to 
ensure compliance with the defined security standards and policies.
3.2.7. Documentation and Training
The final stage of the SSDLC involves preparing comprehensive documentation and conducting training 
sessions for system users and administrators. Documentation will include system design documents, 
technical manuals, user guides, and security policies. The purpose of documentation is to ensure that all 
stakeholders understand how to operate, maintain, and secure the system effectively.
Training sessions will be organized for different user groups — including administrators, billing 
officers, and IT staff — focusing on both system functionality and security awareness. Administrators 
will be trained on managing user accounts, reviewing audit logs, and responding to security alerts. 
Billing officers will learn how to process invoices, verify transactions, and manage customer data 
securely. Customers will receive guidance on safe login practices, password management, and 
recognizing potential phishing attempts.
Moreover, cybersecurity awareness training will be conducted to educate staff on emerging threats such 
as phishing, social engineering, and insider attacks. Regular refresher training will be scheduled to 
maintain high security hygiene across the organization. Documentation will also include an incident 
response plan detailing steps for identifying, reporting, and mitigating security incidents.
By emphasizing training and documentation, Kingstone WiFi Solutions ensures that its staff are not only 
equipped to use the new system effectively but also capable of maintaining its security integrity over 
time.
Conclusion
In conclusion, adopting the Secure Software Development Lifecycle (SSDLC) methodology ensures that 
the Kingstone WiFi Billing System is built with security at its core. Each phase — from planning and 
23
requirement gathering to deployment and training — integrates security measures designed to prevent, 
detect, and respond to threats. By embedding best practices such as encryption, access control, secure 
coding, and continuous monitoring, the project aims to deliver a billing system that is resilient, efficient, 
and trustworthy. Ultimately, this methodology guarantees that the system will not only meet operational 
needs but also uphold the highest standards of data protection, regulatory compliance, and user 
confidence.
